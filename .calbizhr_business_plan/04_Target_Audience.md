## Ideal Customer Profile

BizHR's ideal customer is a California small business with hourly, shift-based employees — the workforce segment carrying the highest wage-and-hour and compliance litigation risk, and the segment least able to afford traditional HR or legal counsel [web:1]. These businesses typically operate with 5–75 employees, sit below the threshold where a full-time HR hire makes financial sense, and have no dedicated compliance function [web:1].

## Named Industry Verticals

BizHR's marketing and product design explicitly target the following industries [web:1]:

- Restaurants, cafes, and boba shops
- Retail storefronts
- Clinics and dental practices
- Salons and spas
- Hospitality (hotels, bars, event venues)
- Home services (cleaning, landscaping, repair)
- Logistics and delivery operations
- Property management
- Childcare and tutoring operations

## High-Stakes Priority Verticals

Beyond the general small-business base, the business plan's cover materials single out three "high-stakes" verticals where regulatory complexity and litigation exposure are most acute, and where BizHR intends to build specialized authority [file:3][file:4]:

| Vertical | Employee range | Primary compliance pressure |
|---|---|---|
| Cannabis (dispensary/cultivation) | 5–50 | Rapid workforce professionalization, novel labor code application, early-stage compliance gaps [file:3] |
| Construction / skilled trades | 5–100 | Worker classification risk, OSHA safety documentation, chronic labor shortages driving AI-assisted recruitment [file:3] |
| Bar / hospitality | 5–50 | SB 553 workplace violence prevention, high-turnover harassment-training gaps [file:3] |

## Buyer Persona

**"The Overextended Owner-Operator"**

- Owns or manages a business with 5–50 employees
- Has no dedicated HR staff; HR duties fall to the owner, office manager, or bookkeeper
- Is aware of general compliance risk but lacks the bandwidth or budget to address it proactively
- Reacts to compliance most often after a triggering event: a new hire's complaint, a Cal/OSHA notice, or hearing about a competitor's lawsuit
- Is price-sensitive but understands that a five-figure settlement is far more expensive than a modest, recurring HR investment [file:3]
- Searches primarily via Google, referrals from accountants/attorneys, and small-business networking groups

## Buying Triggers

- New California mandate taking effect (e.g., SB 553, SB 294, SB 642) creating an urgent compliance deadline [file:7]
- An employee complaint or EEOC/DFEH inquiry
- Rapid headcount growth crossing a legal threshold (5, 15, or 50 employees)
- A competitor or peer business facing litigation, prompting preventive action

## Underserved Segment Rationale

Cannabis operators face a "perfect storm": most are newly licensed, lack legacy HR infrastructure, and operate in an industry still transitioning from informal "startup mode" to structured corporate HR [file:3]. Construction and hospitality businesses similarly combine high regulatory scrutiny with thin administrative staffing, making them receptive to a low-cost, high-trust HR partner rather than a generic national vendor [file:3].
