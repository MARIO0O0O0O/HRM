## Key Assumptions

- Average one-time project fee (Risk Review, Handbook, WVPP build, etc.): $2,100–$2,800 depending on year, based on the $79–$150/hour boutique consulting range applied to typical 15–25 hour engagements [web:18].
- Retainer clients billed at $650/month average, consistent with the lower end of the $1,000–$5,000/month boutique retainer range once volume discounts are applied for smaller businesses [web:15].
- Client acquisition ramps from a single beta client (2026) to a target of 45 active clients by end of Year 3, reflecting the founder's stated goal of full commercial operation by 2027 [web:2].
- Operating expenses include software/AI tool subscriptions, marketing, insurance, and a part-time contractor added in Year 3.

## Revenue Mix Shift

The model assumes a deliberate strategic shift from one-time project work toward recurring retainer revenue — retainer clients grow from 2 (Year 1) to 25 (Year 3), while retainer revenue grows from 12% of total revenue in Year 1 to roughly 76% by Year 3. This mirrors the standard HR-consulting maturity path, where recurring fractional HR contracts provide materially more predictable cash flow than one-off project fees [web:15].

## Break-Even Point

Under these assumptions, BizHR crosses break-even during Year 2, once active clients reach approximately 14–16 and retainer clients reach 5–6, at which point recurring revenue begins covering fixed operating costs independent of new project sales.

## Cost Structure Notes

- Year 1 operating expenses ($26,000) are dominated by AI tool infrastructure, business insurance, and initial marketing/website costs, run largely as a solo operation.
- Year 2 expenses ($52,000) reflect the addition of paid marketing spend and potential part-time administrative support.
- Year 3 expenses ($92,000) assume the first part-time or contract HR associate hire to support delivery capacity beyond the founder alone.

## Sensitivity Considerations

- **Downside case:** If retainer conversion lags (stays below 20% of clients by Year 3), total revenue could fall 25–30% short of the base case, delaying break-even into Year 3.
- **Upside case:** If BizHR successfully penetrates the cannabis vertical as a specialized early mover, average deal size could rise 15–20% given the sector's urgent, underserved compliance need [file:3].
